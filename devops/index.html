Field Notes

The only social cat

Of the world's big cats, the lion alone lives in a permanent group. A pride is built around related females who never leave home, while males come and go after fights won or lost.

A roar with four stages

Recent acoustic studies break the roar into moaning, full roaring, a shorter intermediary roar, and a closing grunt — a sequence, not a single sound, built to travel for miles.

Twenty hours of rest

Lions are built for short, explosive bursts, not stamina. Their hearts are proportionally small for a predator's, so most of the day is spent conserving energy in the shade.

Anatomy of a King

The mane

Advertising, not just armor

The mane is the lion's signature — darker, fuller manes correlate with higher testosterone and greater success holding a pride, but they also cost more in heat stress. It's a walking résumé, read at a distance by rivals and mates alike.

  • Grows in Around adolescence, full size near age four
  • Color range Blonde through black, tinged with rust
  • Function Signals fitness; disputed as neck protection
The build

Muscle over stamina

Nearly 59% of a lion's body weight is skeletal muscle — the highest ratio of any mammal. Fast-twitch fibers deliver a 74 km/h sprint over a few seconds, but the same design means a hunt has to end quickly or not at all.

  • Top speed Recorded near 74 km/h (46 mph)
  • Bite force ~1,594 N at the canines
  • Weight Up to 225 kg for large males

Conservation

Vulnerable, and falling

African lion numbers have declined roughly 43% since the early 1990s, driven mostly by habitat loss and conflict with people. The Asiatic lion survives in one place on Earth — Gir National Park, India — now home to several hundred animals rebuilt from as few as eighteen.
